## IFRS 18 for Insurers: Overview and Implications

Note: this document is an original synthesis prepared for the ValAct_RAG knowledge base, not a reproduction of any single source. It draws on publicly available secondary commentary (cited at the end) because the primary IFRS Foundation standard text is restricted to eIFRS subscribers, which this RAG does not have access to. Treat this as a secondary-source overview, not authoritative standard text — verify against the primary standard before relying on it for a filing position.

### What IFRS 18 is

IFRS 18, "Presentation and Disclosure in Financial Statements," was issued by the International Accounting Standards Board (IASB) in April 2024. It replaces IAS 1 and is effective for annual reporting periods beginning on or after January 1, 2027, with early adoption permitted. Application is retrospective, meaning comparative periods must be restated on transition.

IFRS 18 is a presentation and disclosure standard, not a measurement standard. It does not change how insurers measure their financial performance or their insurance contract liabilities — that remains governed by IFRS 17. It changes how results get displayed and communicated in the financial statements.

### Three changes that matter for insurers

**1. Mandatory income statement structure.** IFRS 18 requires all entities to classify income statement items into five defined categories: operating, investing, financing, income taxes, and discontinued operations. It introduces two new mandatory subtotals: "operating profit" and "profit before financing and income taxes." Previously, IFRS gave companies significant discretion over income statement structure; IFRS 18 removes much of that discretion.

**2. Insurance finance income/expense stays in "operating."** For insurers specifically, IFRS 18 requires that insurance finance income and expense be classified within the operating category, regardless of the insurer's business model — with limited exceptions for equity-method investments. This was a specific point of industry input during the standard's development, since insurers argued their financing-like items function differently from a typical corporate's financing activities.

**3. New disclosure regime for non-GAAP metrics.** IFRS 18 introduces "Management-Defined Performance Measures" (MPMs) as a defined, regulated category. Any non-IFRS metric a company publishes in its external communications (e.g., "adjusted operating income," "core earnings," "underlying profit" — measures insurers commonly report alongside IFRS 17 results) must now be disclosed with: a description, the calculation methodology, a reconciliation to the nearest IFRS-specified subtotal, and the associated tax effects.

### Interaction with IFRS 17

IFRS 17 already prescribes its own insurance-specific income statement line items — insurance revenue, insurance service expense, and insurance finance income/expense. IFRS 18 sits above this as the general presentation framework that applies to all companies, insurers included. In practice, insurers need to map their IFRS 17 line items into IFRS 18's five categories and its two new mandatory subtotals. Because IFRS 18 doesn't touch measurement, the IFRS 17 contract measurement model itself is unaffected — this is a presentation overlay, not a re-opening of IFRS 17.

The practical friction point is timing: insurers only finished transitioning to IFRS 17 (effective 2023) and now face a second major presentation-level change effective 2027, with much of the non-GAAP metric disclosure insurers already publish (embedded value-adjacent measures, "core" or "adjusted" earnings) landing squarely in the new MPM regime.

### What insurers are being told to do now

Secondary commentary (KPMG, May 2026) frames the near-term work as: evaluate how IFRS 18 changes current presentation practice, build a transition plan covering the required restatement of comparatives, and prepare stakeholders — particularly investors and analysts accustomed to existing non-GAAP metrics — for how those metrics will now be presented and reconciled.
