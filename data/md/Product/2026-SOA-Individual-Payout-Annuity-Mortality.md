## Study Highlights

The Society of Actuaries (SOA) Research Institute's Individual Annuity Experience Committee (IAEC) and LIMRA have jointly conducted a mortality experience study of individual payout annuities, covering calendar years 2020 through 2024. The intent of this study is to provide recent annuitant mortality experience, which can be compared to the most recent standard valuation tables and applicable population mortality. Twenty-three parent company groups, comprising 26 individual companies, contributed data to the current study, covering just over 80\% of the industry based on recent sales. Twenty parent company groups encompassing 25 individual companies contributed to the previous study that included calendar years 2014 through 2019 and was completed in 2022. Thirteen parent company groups covering 16 individual companies contributed to both studies.

The current study encompasses 3.1 million contract-years exposed, \$33 billion in annual income-years exposed and 143,190 deaths over a five-year period. The previous study encompassed over 4.3 million contract-years exposed, about \$33.6 billion in annual income-years exposed and over 236,000 deaths over a six-year period. Average annual income increased from \$7,800 to \$11,000. The overall industry figures presented in this paragraph are derived from unscaled industry data to provide a comprehensive view of industry experience. Secondary annuitant results are excluded when the corresponding primary annuitant remains alive.

The overall Actual-to-Expected (A/E) ratio, assuming an expected basis of the 2012 IAM Basic Table on an amount basis, was 99.6\%, down from 104.7\% in the previous study. The overall A/E ratio, assuming an expected basis of the 2012 IAM Basic Table projected with mortality improvement scale G2 on an amount basis, was 108.4\%, remaining generally consistent with the previous study (108.7\%).

In the results split by sex, the amount-based 2012 IAM Basic Table A/E ratios were 100.1\% for Females and 99.1\% for Males. The amount-based 2012 IAM Basic Table with G2 A/E ratios were 107.5\% for Females and 109.4\% for Males.

For five-year Attained Age Groups with credibility of 65\% or more, all ages except 65-74 had 2012 IAM Basic G2 A/E ratios on an amount basis greater than 100\%. The 2012 IAM Basic G2 A/E ratios by amount for Attained Age Groups 65-69 and 70-74 were 80\% and 92\%, respectively.

The A/E ratios by Annual Income Group generally decrease as annual income increases, with the exception that the \$35,000-\$49,999 Annual Income Group exhibits slightly higher A/E ratios than the \$25,000-\$34,999 Annual Income Group. Under the 2012 IAM Basic G2, the A/E ratios on an amount basis ranged from 126\% for Annual Income below \$2,500 to 91.5\% for Annual Income of \$50,000 or more.

## Study Methodology, Reliances and Limitations

Expected deaths were determined by taking the attained age mortality rate in the expected basis table times the exposure in that contract year. For the 2012 IAM Basic G2 Table and the 2012 IAM Period G2 Table A/Es, the 2012 IAM expected mortality rates were improved (or disimproved for A/E ratios for some calendar years in the prior study) from July 1, 2012 to July 1 of the applicable calendar year, and those (improved/disimproved) rates were used to determine the expected deaths for the contract years in that calendar year. The mortality rates in the 2019 SSA Table are the 2019 death probabilities from the 2022 Social Security's Old Age, Survivors, and Disability Insurance Trustees Report. ${ }^{2}$ No improvement was used to determine the A/E ratios based on the 2019 SSA Table. The 2012 IAM Basic and Period Tables were developed on an age nearest birthday basis using amount-weighted experience. Actual study experience was determined on an age-nearest birthday basis.

The study includes immediate annuities, deferred income annuities, settlement options, and annuitizations of life insurance and annuity death claims. To ensure the deaths are reliable, the data reflects annuitants whose payments are life contingent now or will be sometime in the future. Certain period-only non-life contingent annuities are excluded. The request for data that went out to companies included standard annuities but excluded substandard annuities due to the paucity of substandard data. For joint life annuities, the request also included experience data on both lives throughout the contract to enable enhanced experience analysis of the secondary annuitant.

The exposure for joint lives is determined consistently with the approach in the prior study where no recognition is given to the secondary annuitant if alive while the primary annuitant is alive. This was done because of concerns about the under-reporting of deaths on the secondary annuitants when both annuitants are alive.

[^0]Structured settlement annuities are excluded from this report, as the mortality experience on this specialized block has recently been compiled and studied separately; a report on structured settlement mortality experience was published in January 2020 covering 2005 through 2017.

No assessment has been made concerning the applicability of this experience for other purposes. In developing this report, the SOA Research Institute and LIMRA relied upon data and information supplied by the participating company contributors. For each contributor, this information includes, but is not limited to, the data submission for mortality experience and the responses to follow-up questions.

The results in this report are technical in nature and are dependent on certain assumptions and methods. No party should rely upon these results without a thorough understanding of those assumptions and methods. Such an understanding may require consultation with qualified professionals. This report should be distributed and reviewed only in its entirety.
